01 — Anatomy of a sales story

Four beats, one ask

Every story in this page follows the same spine. Keep the beats tight and the ask soft — the story does the persuading.

01

Situation

Set the scene in one sentence. Name the peer's world: "A {industry} org your size..." Not their name, not their company.

02

Struggle

The honest problem — the one that mirrors theirs. Struggle is what creates the listener's "that's me" moment.

03

Shift

What changed. One action, one tool, one decision. Avoid a feature list — describe the before/after, not the packaging.

04

Success + Ask

The measured outcome, then a question that hands the story to them: "Sound familiar? What's that costing you now?"

02 — Delivery

How to tell it without turning it into a pitch

45–90 seconds

Long enough to land, short enough to leave them wanting the detail. If it runs longer, cut the middle, keep the before/after.

One concrete number

Completion climbed, attrition dropped, the launch shipped — one number beats five adjectives. Stats support the story; they don't replace it.

End with their mirror

The last line is never about us. It's a question that pulls their version of the story out. Then listen — the next objection or need is usually inside their answer.

Confidentiality rule: anonymize always — "a {industry} org," "a CTO we worked with," "a peer of yours." If you can name a client with permission, the story gets stronger; without it, the anonymized version is the only version that exists.
