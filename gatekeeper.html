01 — Know the gate

The AI listens for four things — give it the right four

Screening AI is not a person and not a wall. It is a router with four fields to fill: name, company, reason, intent. Fill the fields the way the DM's office fills them, and the router sends you where a human would.

01

The name

"Who's calling? Who are you asking for?" No decision-maker name means the general queue. Say your name and theirs inside the first six words.

02

The reason

It scores your reason and routes on keywords. "Training" lands in the vendor queue; "readiness," "capacity," "evidence" route to the department. Your first sentence is the routing map.

03

The escape routes

It offers voicemail, message-taking and call-backs. Each is a deflection with one recoverable move: hand it a deliverable with a name and a number, not a vague hope.

04

The summary

The AI forwards a transcript line to the DM's team when it routes. Your opening sentence is your first impression — write it like a subject line, not a monologue.

02 — Recovery

When the gate deflects, recover with a deliverable

The voicemail offer

Take it — but on your terms: a callback date and the deliverable named. "Have {Name} call me this week; the note makes the reason obvious in one line." A voicemail with a file behind it gets returned.

The message trap

"I'll take a message" dead-ends unless the message is a file. "The sample is ready to send — {Name} can judge it from the data." Turn the trap into a hand-off of evidence, not a promise of a future call.

The meeting block

"They're in a meeting" can last forever. Pivot the recipient instead of waiting: "Then 15 minutes with the chief-of-staff or the L&D lead is equally useful." A second route beats a calendar wait.

The recovery rule inside every script above: name the decision-maker, name the deliverable you are carrying, and give a number. Those three give the gate something concrete to route — everything else is what gets screened out.
