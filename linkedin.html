LinkedIn messages that earn the reply — ten per level, ≤300 characters.

Pick the decision-maker level and get ten LinkedIn touches each — a connection-request note that fits the character limit, plus the follow-up message to send once they accept. Profile triggers, voicemail and email replays, engagement on their post, the graceful breakup and the windshield close. The connection note opens the room; the message earns the reply.

01 — Before you send

Three rules of the first note

LinkedIn punishment is quick: notes that pitch get declined, people who pitch in comments get muted. The note opens a door; the message carries the value.

01

Note << pitch

≤300 characters, one reason to connect, zero asks. "Happy to connect" or "no pitch, just context" — the pitch lives in the message after they accept.

02

Message within 24h

The accept is a window, not an open door. Send the follow-up message the same day — a warm accept that goes two weeks unanswered turns cold fast.

03

One CTA, easy to decline

"Reply and it's yours" or "One reply either way and I'm out of your inbox" — a graceful exit is what makes a second touch feel respectful, not stalker-ish.

02 — Notes that get accepted

Write like a peer, not a connection farmer

Named, not sprayed

Their name, their company, their trigger. A note with zero specifics reads like it went to 400 people — it gets declined at the same speed.

Say what's in it

"Sharing useful context" or "the reporting sample" — the recipient wants to know what accepting gets them before they tap.

Respect the 300

LinkedIn truncates the connection note. Keep the hook and the promise in the first two lines; cut the adjectives.

No pitch in comments

Engage on their post genuinely first. The DM comes after the comment is welcome — never "great post, let me tell you about our training."

03 — Personalize

Swap the placeholders before you send

TokenReplace with
One real detail beats three placeholders. Their actual post, their real new role, the mutual connection's name — pick the detail that proves you looked, then the note writes itself.
